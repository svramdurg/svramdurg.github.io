*Public internet involves users moving to a public place to use internet
by paying a small price for the internet used. Public internet is
popular in many advanced economies. Why is public internet not gaining
traction in India while mobile and home internet are flourishing? The
Government's PM-WANI public WiFi scheme aims to make public internet
popular by promoting public WiFi hotspots through private participation.
There seems to exist lot of private and public interest for this scheme
to advance and succeed, however very less PM-WANI public WiFi hotspots
are observed on ground. The reasons are worth exploring.*

**Justification for public internet**

Mobile internet seems to be satisfying the basic internet user
requirements to a large extent; however specific user requirements may
still not be met.

Choice of internet depends on the quality (speed and response) of
internet promised by the internet provider in internet plans. The
quality promised in the plans are usually loosely defined because
networks are spatially deployed, capacity constrained and cannot meet
strict quality requirements for all users simultaneously when loaded.
Mobile networks are most prone to not deliver quality internet due to
dynamically changing radio links and network loads caused by moving
users. So, mobile internet users though seem satisfied for basic
requirements may be challenged as follows, thus justifying need for
public internet.

-  <u> Users in transit</u>: When not satisfied with mobile
    internet in transit, they look for public internet with required and
    reliable quality usable on pay as you go basis.

-   <u>Users at home</u>: When not satisfied with mobile internet
    quality at home, they opt for home internet provided using a
    dedicated wired (telephone, cable, fiber, etc.) or wireless
    (wireless local loop, satellite etc.) link. Home internet creates a
    personal hot spot at home for the user typically promising more
    reliable internet quality for the paid price. Home internet comes at
    a higher price than mobile internet and may not be affordable for
    all. Users who cannot afford a private home internet, look for
    public internet with required quality near homes usable on a pay as
    you go basis.

**Reasons for low adoption of public internet**

The public in general are not aware about public internet as an option
for obtaining internet services in public places at reasonable prices.
Such options were available in 1990's and 2000's in the form of internet
browsing centres but have now been forgotten in the world of mobile
internet. The internet providers are benefitted due to this low
awareness as it allows them to continue promoting mobile and home
internet services which are personal services and are less demanding on
resources of the internet provider. Public internet is more demanding on
resources as it requires providing enterprise grade internet and the
connection is prone to high internet utilization due to resource sharing
by many simultaneous users. The economic calculations of providing
public internet thus keep internet providers away from promoting public
internet. This decision of internet providers is sustainable because of
less competition due to monopoly of few internet providers in delivering
all of enterprise, mobile and home internet services.

The governments intervention by launching the PM-WANI scheme attempts to
promote public internet by involving private "aggregators" providing
hardware and software infrastructure for user authentication, admission
and accounting to the small shop owners deploying PM-WANI hot spots.
Even in this arrangement, the aggregators and shop owners as private
players are together unable to influence the internet provider to reduce
price of enterprise internet. The Government many times tries to
intervene to cap the enterprise internet pricing for PM-WANI, however
this seems to have little effect on the internet providers due to the
market power they enjoy.

**Proposal to improve adoption of public internet**

The way forward requires creating a competitive free market for public
internet to exist in parallel to existing market for mobile and home
internet.

To do this, government may want to reduce interventions and increase
scheme visibility and branding by (i) Modifying the PM-WANI architecture
to keep the internet provider in central role by absorbing the
aggregator's role (ii) Letting PM-WANI user requests be directly handled
by internet provider to understand public WiFi market demand (iii)
Allowing internet providers to come up with innovative revenue sharing
business models for the enterprise internet connections specific to
PM-WANI (iv) Allowing shop owners to directly work with internet
providers to adopt a suitable business model for setup of PM-WANI
hotspots (v) Not putting any price caps on enterprise internet or retail
internet and allowing the prices to settle down based on market demand
and supply (vi) Make PM-WANI hotspots prominently visible with name
boards like "PM-WANI Public WiFi" at PM-WANI hotspots across India (vii)
Pilot the scheme and create competition in rural areas first where the
need for public internet is expected to be more due to low mobile
internet quality and low economic status of people making private home
internet connections unaffordable.

**Conclusion**

Public internet does seem to have a market in India whenever mobile
internet fails to deliver the required internet quality. However,
limited user awareness, self-interest of monopolist internet providers,
and more than needed government interventions have resulted in low
adoption of public internet. Some minor changes in architecture of
PM-WANI scheme and increased visibility of the scheme through
appropriate branding may help to create the needed incentives to stake
holders to make the scheme viable and successful.
