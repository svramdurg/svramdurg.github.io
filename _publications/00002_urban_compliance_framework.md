excerpt: 'A major portion of urban populations live in collectively owned residential complexes (such as Resident Welfare Associations / RWAs). However, compliance with environmental and safety regulations remains low due to fragmented agency rules, short-term management committee turnover, narrow ROI interpretations of compliance spending, and lack of digital tracking infrastructure.   This paper identifies key behavioral, economic, and institutional drivers of urban non-compliance and proposes the Unified Urban Environment and Safety Monitoring/Tracking System (UESMS). The framework introduces licensed Urban Compliance Officers as independent domain experts to manage compliance returns, along with a harmonized digital infrastructure featuring standardized applicability matrices, automated fine calculators, star-rating incentives, and whistleblower e-boxes'
